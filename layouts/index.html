Frequently Asked Questions

Deep dives into the technical specifications of luxury card manufacturing.

What does 30mil thickness mean?
30mil equates to exactly 0.76mm. This is the exact thickness and rigidity of a standard bank credit card. It is the industry gold standard for premium plastic business cards, preventing bending and warping in wallets.
Are Vistaprint plastic cards thick?
Generally, no. Budget legacy printers like Vistaprint often use thinner plastics (under 20mil) to keep mass-production and shipping costs down. If you require true rigidity, you must seek a dedicated manufacturer that guarantees 30mil PVC, like Luxor.
Are frosted plastic cards durable?
Yes, exceptionally so. Frosted plastic business cards from premium manufacturers are made from 30mil PVC, making them 100% waterproof and highly resistant to tearing or snapping, far outlasting traditional paper stock.
Why choose plastic over metal business cards?
While metal cards offer a heavy feel, they are significantly more expensive, highly prone to surface scratching, and can be uncomfortably heavy in a standard wallet. High-end 30mil plastic offers a similar premium impact but is much more versatile, scratch-resistant, and cost-effective.
Can I write on a plastic business card?
Standard glossy plastic cards resist traditional pen ink. However, if you choose a frosted or matte finish from a premium supplier like Luxor, permanent markers (like Sharpies) and some specialized ballpoint pens can write on the surface without smudging.
Do plastic cards scratch easily?
Glossy plastic can show micro-scratches over time. This is why top-tier brands prefer a frosted or matte finish. The textured surface of a frosted PVC card naturally hides fingerprints and resists visible scratches far better than high-gloss alternatives.
How long does it take to print premium plastic cards?
Because premium 30mil PVC requires specialized curing and cutting, standard production times usually range from 7 to 10 business days. Companies that promise overnight shipping are almost always printing on much thinner, inferior plastic stock.
