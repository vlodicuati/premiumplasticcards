## Understanding the Measurements: Points (pt) vs. Mils (mil)

When ordering business cards, buyers are frequently confused by the terminology used by print vendors. Paper thickness is measured in "Points" (pt), while plastic thickness is measured in "Mils".

*   **14pt Paper:** Measures 0.014 inches (0.35 mm). This is the baseline budget card stock used by legacy mass-printers. It is highly flexible and creases easily <a href="#ref1" class="text-blue-600 no-underline hover:underline"><sup>[1]</sup></a>.
*   **16pt Paper:** Measures 0.016 inches (0.41 mm). Often advertised as "premium" paper, it feels slightly stiffer in hand but remains susceptible to edge fraying and moisture damage.
*   **30mil PVC:** Measures 0.030 inches (0.76 mm). This is the exact global ISO 7810 standard used for manufacturing bank credit cards <a href="#ref2" class="text-blue-600 no-underline hover:underline"><sup>[2]</sup></a>. **It is literally twice as thick as a standard 14pt paper card.**

## The Problem with Paper: Wallet Friction and Degradation

Paper business cards are engineered for temporary use. If you attend a massive trade show and need to hand out 500 cards in an hour, 14pt paper is the most cost-effective solution. 

However, if your business card is meant to close a high-ticket sale, paper presents severe flaws:

1. **Moisture Vulnerability:** A single drop of condensation from a glass or a humid day can warp the paper fibers and cause ink to bleed. 
2. **Wallet Fraying:** When placed in a tight wallet, the friction against leather and other cards causes 14pt and 16pt paper edges to separate, fray, and peel. Handing a worn-out card to a prospect actively damages your brand perception.
3. **The "Throwaway" Psychology:** Because paper is cheap and ubiquitous, clients feel no psychological resistance to throwing paper cards in the recycling bin within 24 hours of receiving them.

  <p id="ref3"><strong>[3] ASTM D570: Standard Test Method for Water Absorption of Plastics:</strong> Polyvinyl Chloride (PVC) structural testing proves near-zero percent moisture absorption compared to porous cellulose-based paper products. <a href="https://www.astm.org/d0570-98r18.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="text-blue-600 hover:underline">View ASTM Documentation</a></p>
