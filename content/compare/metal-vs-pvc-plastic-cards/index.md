## The "Heavy" Debate: Perception vs. Practicality

When upgrading to a VIP business card, executives typically narrow their choices down to two premium materials: Stainless Steel/Aluminum or 30mil Solid PVC.

The appeal of metal is obvious: it is heavy and cold to the touch. Handing someone a metal card feels like handing them a gadget. However, as a functional daily networking tool and brand asset, metal introduces severe print limitations and physical flaws that undermine your brand image.

## The Flaws of Metal Business Cards

Before investing $3.00 to $6.00 *per card* on metal printing, it is critical to understand the engineering and design drawbacks of the medium:

1. **Strict Color Limits & Dull Saturation:** Metal cannot hold complex offset CMYK ink layers cleanly. Most metal cards are restricted to simple laser etching, monochrome cut-outs, or basic 1-to-2 spot silk-screen colors. High-resolution photo gradients, rich dark backgrounds, and bright branded logos are virtually impossible to reproduce accurately on raw metal without the coating flaking off.
2. **The Scratch Factor:** Painted or anodized metal cards (especially in black, matte navy, or gold) scratch easily. Sliding a metal card in and out of a leather wallet alongside coins strips the topcoat, revealing the raw silver steel underneath within weeks.
3. **Impractical Weight:** A single stainless steel card weighs roughly 20-25 grams. Carrying just 10 of these adds half a pound of sharp metal to your suit pocket or handbag.
4. **NFC / RFID Interference:** Metal acts as a Faraday cage. If you want to embed an NFC chip so clients can tap your card to their smartphone, solid metal actively blocks and kills the radio frequency signal.
