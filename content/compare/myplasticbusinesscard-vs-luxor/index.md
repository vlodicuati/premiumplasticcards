## The Problem with "Clear" Plastic: Haze and Micro-Scratches

Creating a truly transparent plastic business card is one of the most difficult engineering challenges in commercial printing. It requires absolute control over the lamination process and the environment.

When assessing **MyPlasticBusinessCard (MPBC)**, their primary reputation stems from metal manufacturing. When they adapted their pipeline to include plastic, they applied their luxury price tags—but our audits show the material quality often doesn't match the cost.

**The "Cloudy" Issue:**
Many buyers of expensive clear cards are disappointed to find that the plastic is not actually "crystal clear." Instead, the cards exhibit a milky, cloudy film (known in material science as **"Haze"**) <a href="#ref1" class="text-blue-600 no-underline hover:underline"><sup>[1]</sup></a>. This happens when printers use cheaper extruded plastics or fail to use optical-grade lamination overlays, resulting in micro-abrasions that scatter light instead of letting it pass cleanly through.
